# The Partner Lifecycle

The spine that connects everything in this plugin. Six stages, each with entry criteria, the work
that happens inside it, exit criteria, and the primary owner. Most partner programs fail not in
one stage but in the **handoffs between stages**: a qualified partner who never gets onboarded, a
signed JBP that no co-sell motion ever acts on. Watch the seams.

Use this to locate where a given partner is, then run the skill or agent that owns that
stage. A partner is rarely in one clean stage; place them by their weakest completed exit gate.

---

## 1. Recruit

**Entry:** a defined ideal-partner profile and a target list worth pursuing.
**Work:** source and prioritize candidates against the IPP; make the build/partner/buy call; open
the conversation with a specific joint thesis, not a generic "let's partner."
**Exit:** a shortlist of candidates that clear the IPP bar and a first meeting booked.
**Owns it:** `partner-strategist` (the thesis and the target set) and `partner-researcher` (the
evidence on each candidate) · the `partner-strategy` skill, the `research` skill.

> The most common failure here is chasing logos. Partner count is a weak signal. A shortlist of
> five partners you can activate beats fifty you can't.

## 2. Qualify

**Entry:** a named candidate and enough evidence to score them.
**Work:** score the candidate against the IPP; run the anti-fit / where-we-won't-play gate; decide
go / no-go / not-yet with the reason stated.
**Exit:** a go/no-go decision with a documented rationale. If go, a tier hypothesis.
**Owns it:** `partner-qualifier` · the `qualify` skill · the `partner-brief` skill for the deep dive.

> Qualification is a commercial filter, not a courtesy. "Not yet" is a real and useful answer:
> most partners fail because they were activated before either side was ready.

## 3. Onboard

**Entry:** a go decision and, ideally, a signed joint business plan with named exec sponsors.
**Work:** the first 90 days, a JBP that both sides own, onboarding milestones, first enablement,
first joint pipeline. Turn a signature into a producing partner.
**Exit:** the partner has completed onboarding milestones and has a first qualified opportunity in
motion.
**Owns it:** `partner-enablement-lead` · the `onboard` skill · `partner-strategist` designs the path,
`cosell-lead` takes the first deals.

> The 90-day window is where partnerships are won or lost. A partner who produces nothing in the
> first quarter rarely recovers momentum.

## 4. Co-sell

**Entry:** an onboarded partner and at least one live opportunity.
**Work:** account mapping, joint close plans, rules of engagement, deal registration, and the
field-level intel/influence/intros motion. Keep both sales teams aligned on who does what.
**Exit:** repeatable joint pipeline and a clean attribution trail (sourced / influenced / co-sold).
**Owns it:** `cosell-lead` · the `cosell-plan` skill · load the `co-sell-motion` skill.

> Co-sell is an incentives problem before it is a tooling problem. If the field ignores the
> partner, look at comp and routing before you look at the PRM.

## 5. Grow

**Entry:** a partner producing repeatable pipeline.
**Work:** QBRs against the JBP, scorecards, tier reviews, expansion into new motions or segments,
economics review (rev-share, MDF ROI, partner-sourced contribution).
**Exit:** the partner is expanding, more pipeline, more surface area, higher tier, or is flagged
for the renew/prune decision.
**Owns it:** `partner-performance` · the `qbr-prep` skill · load `partner-economics`.

> Ecosystem value is usually clear at leadership level and muddy in the field. Growth is where you
> make it legible: sourced vs. influenced, activated vs. signed, real contribution vs. loose
> attribution.

## 6. Renew / Prune

**Entry:** a renewal window or a persistent underperformance signal.
**Work:** renewal readiness and advocacy for the performers; the honest demote-or-exit call for
the rest. Communicate the decision, wind down cleanly, reallocate the investment.
**Exit:** a renewed and re-committed partnership, or a clean, well-communicated exit.
**Owns it:** `partner-performance` (decision) and `partner-communicator` (the message) · the `qbr-prep` and
`partner-comms` skills.

> Pruning is a portfolio discipline, not a failure. Every hour spent propping up a dead partner is
> an hour not spent on one that could produce. Say the hard thing early and cleanly.

## Stage to skill map

| Stage | Workflow skill | Agent | Knowledge skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recruit | the `research` skill, the `partner-strategy` skill | `partner-researcher`, `partner-strategist` | `partner-research`, `partner-frameworks` |
| Qualify | the `qualify` skill, the `partner-brief` skill | `partner-qualifier` | `partner-frameworks` |
| Onboard | the `onboard` skill, the `program-design` skill | `partner-enablement-lead`, `partner-strategist` | `program-architecture` |
| Co-sell | the `cosell-plan` skill | `cosell-lead` | `co-sell-motion` |
| Grow | the `qbr-prep` skill | `partner-performance` | `partner-economics` |
| Renew / Prune | the `qbr-prep` skill, the `partner-comms` skill | `partner-performance`, `partner-communicator` | `partner-economics` |

Four skills sit across the whole spine rather than inside one stage:

| Skill | Agent | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| the `partner-os` skill | none (routes directly) | You don't know where a partner stands or which skill fits |
| the `partner-maturity-check` skill, the `diagnose` skill | `partner-diagnostician` | The function is underperforming, or one partner has stopped converting |
| the `partner-health-check` skill | `partner-performance` | You want a quick read on one partner |
| the `partner-numbers` skill | none (the connector) | The question has a number in it |

**Motion type cuts across every stage.** A referral partner and a services SI move through these six
stages differently: different activation definitions, different economics, sometimes no co-sell
stage at all. The `partner-motions` skill carries the deltas; load it once the type is known.
