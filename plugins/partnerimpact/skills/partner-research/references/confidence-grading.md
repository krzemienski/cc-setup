# Confidence Grading

Every claim in an evidence log carries a grade. The grade is not decoration. It decides whether a
qualification score can rest on the claim, and it is the difference between research and assertion.

## The grades

### A: verified
Stated directly by a primary source, or corroborated across two independent sources.

Examples: a named partner listed in the company's own partner directory; an integration documented
in their API reference; a partner-director role open on their careers page.

An A-grade claim can carry a scoring dimension on its own.

### B: indicated
One credible source, uncorroborated, or a direct statement from a promotional source.

Examples: a partnership named only in a press release; a marketplace listing with no supporting
documentation; a customer logo with no case study behind it.

A B-grade claim can support a score alongside something else. It cannot carry a dimension alone.

### C: inferred
A reasonable reading of indirect evidence. No source states it.

Examples: "they probably sell mid-market" inferred from pricing page tiers; "the program looks
dormant" inferred from a stale partner page.

A C-grade claim is a hypothesis to test with the user. It never carries a score. Write it as the
inference it is, "no partner content since 2024 suggests the program is dormant", never as
"the program is dormant."

## The wall

Four things public evidence cannot establish, at any grade. Do not infer across this line; name the
gap and say who would know.

**Economics.** Their margin, their willingness on rev-share, what a deal costs to serve.
Nothing public tells you this. → Their partner lead, in conversation.

**Executive commitment.** Whether someone senior will own the partnership, or whether
it's a logo exercise. A partner-director hire is a B-grade proxy at best. → A sponsor conversation.

**Delivery quality.** Whether they can implement, support, and retain the customer. Case studies
select for success; review sites select for complaint. → Reference calls with shared customers.

**Channel conflict with your direct motion.** Whether they compete with you where it matters
depends entirely on how *you* sell, which is not public at all. → The user, and their sales
leadership.

There is a fifth, quieter one: **whether they want to partner with you specifically.** Their
ecosystem tells you they partner. It does not tell you they will prioritize you.

## Writing the log

- One claim per line, with source and grade.
- Absence is a finding. "No partner directory found" is an A-grade claim about their program.
- Never average grades into an overall confidence score. A log with one decisive A and nine Cs is
  not "mostly C". It is one solid fact and a lot of speculation, and the reader needs to see that
  shape.
- If the log is mostly C, say so at the top and recommend a conversation before a scoring exercise.
  Scoring thin evidence produces a precise-looking number built on nothing.
