# Evidence Sourcing

Where to look, in descending order of what the source is worth. The ranking matters: a company's
partner directory is a fact about their ecosystem, and their homepage is a fact about their
marketing.

## Tier 1: structural evidence

Hard to stage, expensive to fake, changes slowly.

### Their partner directory or partner page
The single most valuable page. Tells you whether a program exists at all, what tiers they use, who
is in them, and how recently anyone was added. A partner page with twelve logos and no tier
structure is a logo wall, not a program.

**Read for:** named partners, tier names, program requirements, application process, whether
partners are searchable or just displayed, last-updated signals.

### Marketplace and app-store listings
Their own listing on someone else's marketplace tells you what they've built and what
they're willing to maintain. Listings on AWS, Azure, Salesforce, HubSpot, Shopify and the like each
imply a real integration and a real commercial agreement behind them.

**Read for:** which marketplaces they're on, listing quality and recency, review volume, whether
the listing is transactable or a directory entry, what integrations they claim.

### Integration and developer documentation
Public API docs, connector lists, and changelogs are the least promotional thing a company
publishes. A connector list is a factual statement about who they interoperate with.

**Read for:** named integrations, depth (webhook vs. full bidirectional sync), which ones get
changelog attention, whether they run a developer program.

## Tier 2: investment evidence

Shows where money and headcount are going.

### Job posts
The most underrated partner signal. A company hiring a "Director of Partnerships" is committing
budget; a company with three open partner-manager roles across regions is building a function. No
partner roles in eighteen months means partnerships are somebody's side project.

**Read for:** partner-titled roles open now, seniority, region, how the role describes the motion
(recruit vs. co-sell vs. technical), and reporting line where stated.

### Leadership and org signals
Who owns partnerships and where they sit. A partner lead reporting to the CRO is a different
company from one reporting to a product director.

**Read for:** partner leadership hires and departures, reporting line, whether the exec team
mentions partnerships in its own positioning.

## Tier 3: narrative evidence

Useful, but authored to persuade. Corroborate before relying on it.

### Case studies and joint content
A joint case study means two marketing teams cooperated and a customer agreed. That's real, but it
selects for the best outcome that partnership ever produced.

**Read for:** named joint customers, which partners get co-marketing effort, deal shapes, whether
the same three references recur (a thin partnership dressed up).

### Press and announcements
Partnership announcements are cheap to issue and rarely followed up. Value is in the pattern, not
the individual release: five announcements and no subsequent joint activity is a signal in itself.

**Read for:** announcement cadence, whether announced partnerships appear in the directory later,
funding and expansion news that changes their priorities.

### Pricing and packaging pages
Whether partners appear in how they sell: implementation partners, referral discounts, reseller
pricing, marketplace-only SKUs.

**Read for:** partner-mediated purchase paths, services attach, discount structures visible to the
public.

## Tier 4: ambient

Community forums, review sites, conference sponsor lists, user groups. Occasionally the only place
a real complaint about delivery quality surfaces. Grade accordingly and never let it carry a claim
on its own.

---

## Working the sources

1. **Resolve the company first.** Right name, right entity, right domain. Everything downstream is
   worthless if this is wrong.
2. **Directory and marketplace before homepage.** Start with what's structural.
3. **Corroborate across tiers.** A claim from Tier 1 confirmed by Tier 2 is an A. A Tier 3 claim
   alone is a C.
4. **Note absence.** No partner page, no integration docs, no partner hires. That is evidence, and
   it is often the most decisive evidence you will find. Record it as a finding, not a blank.
5. **Date everything.** A thriving partner program in 2023 and silence since is a different story
   from a thriving one now.
