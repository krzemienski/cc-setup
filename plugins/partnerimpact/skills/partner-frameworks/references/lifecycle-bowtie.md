# Partner Lifecycle Bowtie

The partner journey modeled as a bowtie, so you manage the *conversion* between stages, not just
the stages. Each transition has one governing metric. When a program stalls, it's almost always at
one specific conversion: find it, fix that metric, don't reflow the whole thing.

The bowtie mirrors the six-stage lifecycle in `references/partner-lifecycle.md`, but adds the
conversion rate and the leading/lagging KPI at each seam.

```
   RECRUIT → QUALIFY → ONBOARD →  [ ACTIVATION POINT ]  → CO-SELL → GROW → RENEW/PRUNE
   (acquire the right partners)         first deal          (produce & expand)
```

The knot in the middle is the **activation point**: first qualified joint opportunity. Everything
left of it is acquisition cost; everything right of it is return. Programs are won or lost on how
fast partners cross it.

## The conversions and their governing metric

| Transition | Governing metric | Healthy signal |
|---|---|---|
| Recruit → Qualify | IPP pass rate | You're sourcing partners that clear the bar, not chasing logos |
| Qualify → Onboard | Go-decision → JBP-signed rate | Qualified partners get committed, not shelved |
| Onboard → Activation | Time-to-first-opportunity | Days from signed JBP to first qualified joint deal |
| Activation → Co-sell | Activation rate | % of onboarded partners with ≥1 qualified opportunity |
| Co-sell → Grow | Repeat-pipeline rate | % of activated partners producing a second and third deal |
| Grow → Renew | Net partner retention / expansion | Producing partners expanding vs. flat vs. declining |

## Leading vs. lagging KPIs

Manage the leading indicators; report the lagging ones.

- **Leading** (predict outcomes): partners onboarded on time, joint account maps completed, deals
  registered, QBRs held on cadence, enablement completion.
- **Lagging** (prove outcomes): partner-sourced revenue, partner-influenced revenue, activation
  rate, net partner retention, partner-sourced pipeline coverage.

## Attribution categories (keep these clean)

Loose attribution is how partner revenue gets inflated and then disbelieved. Hold the line:

- **Sourced**: the partner originated the opportunity. They brought it.
- **Influenced**: the partner materially advanced an opportunity you already had.
- **Co-sold**: both sides actively worked the deal to close.
- **Direct**: no partner involvement. Don't let it drift into "influenced."

## Using the bowtie to diagnose a stall

1. Compute each conversion rate from whatever data the user can paste.
2. Find the worst conversion relative to a healthy benchmark.
3. That seam is the constraint. Everything upstream of it is being wasted.
4. Fix the metric that governs *that* seam before touching anything else.

> Example: high recruit volume, low activation rate → you're acquiring partners you can't onboard.
> The fix is onboarding capacity and time-to-first-opportunity, not more recruitment.
