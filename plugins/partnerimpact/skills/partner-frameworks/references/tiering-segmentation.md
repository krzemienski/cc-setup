# Tiering & Segmentation

Two related but distinct jobs. **Segmentation** groups partners by how you go to market with them.
**Tiering** sets how much you invest in each partner. Confusing the two produces the classic
mistake: treating a high-potential partner in the wrong motion, or pouring top-tier investment into
a partner that will never return it.

## Segmentation: the two-axis model

Segment on **motion type** (how the partner creates value) × **fit score** (from the IPP).

Motion types (adapt to your ecosystem):

- **Referral / agency**: sends leads, light technical involvement.
- **Reseller / VAR**: owns the transaction and often first-line support.
- **Technology / integration**: builds product-level integration; value is in the joint solution.
- **Marketplace**: distribution through a platform's listing and discovery.
- **Services / SI**: implements and drives adoption; owns delivery.
- **Strategic alliance**: deep, multi-motion, exec-sponsored, co-invested.

Each motion has a different value pattern, so each earns a different playbook. A referral partner
scored 85 and an integration partner scored 85 are both excellent, and managed completely
differently. Segment first, then tier within segment.

## Tiering: the points-based model

Tier on demonstrated and potential value. A neutral default: score partners on a small set of
weighted signals, then cut into three tiers. Recalibrate the thresholds to your base: tiers are
relative, not absolute.

> **These are defaults.** If the user already runs tiers, use their tier names, cuts and signals
> instead, and say in one line which values were theirs and which were defaults.

| Signal | What it measures | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| Partner-sourced revenue (TTM) | Proven commercial contribution | 30% |
| Qualified pipeline generated | Forward-looking production | 20% |
| Activation & certification | Enablement completion, active reps | 15% |
| Strategic value | Market access, roadmap alignment, reference value | 20% |
| Commitment | Exec sponsor, dedicated headcount, co-investment | 15% |

Cut into tiers by percentile of your base, not fixed numbers:

| Tier | Rough cut | What it earns |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (Strategic)** | Top ~10–15% | Named partner manager, joint business plan, MDF, exec sponsorship, quarterly QBR |
| **Tier 2 (Managed)** | Next ~25–35% | Shared partner manager, lighter JBP, some MDF, half-yearly review |
| **Tier 3 (Scaled / self-serve)** | The long tail | Digital enablement, portal, community, automated touch: no dedicated human |

## The rule that keeps tiering honest

**Investment follows contribution, but a tier is a bet with an exit.** A partner promoted to Tier 1
on potential has a window to produce. If they don't, demote them: cleanly, with the reason stated.
Tiering only works if the door swings both ways. A base where nobody is ever demoted is a base
where Tier 1 stops meaning anything.

## Promotion / demotion triggers

- **Promote** when a partner sustains production above their tier's threshold for two consecutive
  review periods, or lands a strategic proof point (marquee joint win, deep integration shipped).
- **Demote** when production falls below threshold for two periods with no credible recovery plan,
  or the commitment signal collapses (sponsor leaves, headcount pulled).
- **Review on cadence, not on emotion.** Tier changes happen at the QBR/review, against the model,
  not because a partner had a good call or a bad quarter.

## Output of a tiering exercise

1. Each partner placed by segment and tier, with the points that put them there.
2. The investment and cadence each tier earns (from the table above, calibrated).
3. Any promotion/demotion flags with the trigger that fired.
4. A one-line note on where the user should recalibrate the thresholds for their base.
