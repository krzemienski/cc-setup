# Rev-share & Margin

The money that changes hands between you and a partner. Structure it wrong and you either can't
attract partners (too little for them) or you erode your own economics (too much for you). The
structure should match how the partner creates value: a referral partner and a reseller earn very
differently because they do very different work and carry very different cost.

## Structures by partner type

Neutral defaults: calibrate to your gross margin and the partner's actual cost to serve.

| Partner type | Typical structure | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Referral / agency** | Flat referral fee or % of first-year (often 10–20%) | Low effort, low cost to serve. They hand off and you close |
| **Reseller / VAR** | Margin/discount off list (often 20–40%) | They own the transaction, first-line support, and working capital |
| **Technology / integration** | Rev-share on joint/attributed revenue, or mutual referral | Value is in the joint solution; often reciprocal |
| **Marketplace** | Platform take rate (often 15–30%) reduces your net | You trade margin for distribution and reduced CAC |
| **Services / SI** | Their services margin + your product margin; sometimes resell | They earn on implementation; you keep product economics |

Ranges are illustrative, not gospel. The right number is the one where both sides make money at
realistic volume and neither is subsidizing the other.

## Partner CAC vs. blended CAC: the real question

The channel is only worth it if partner-sourced revenue is acquired more efficiently than direct.
Test it:

```
Partner CAC = (partner program cost + rev-share/margin given + MDF) ÷ partner-sourced new revenue
```

Compare to blended CAC (or direct CAC). Three outcomes:

- **Partner CAC < direct CAC**: the channel is more efficient. Invest.
- **Partner CAC ≈ direct CAC**: the channel buys *reach*, not efficiency. Justify it on access to
  accounts you can't reach direct, not on cost.
- **Partner CAC > direct CAC**: the channel is more expensive per dollar. It can still be right for
  strategic reach, but name that trade-off explicitly; don't pretend it's cheaper.

Most partner programs never run this math. Running it is how you defend the budget to a CFO.

## Margin discipline

- **Model the fully-loaded cost**: rev-share/margin *plus* MDF *plus* program headcount *plus*
  enablement. Partners are rarely as cheap as the headline margin suggests.
- **Protect direct pricing**: layered partner discounts that undercut your direct list erode
  pricing power across the board. Registration and pricing rules exist to prevent this.
- **Watch margin stacking**: multiple discounts (tier + deal reg + SPIFF) compounding on one deal.

## Output of an economics model

1. The rev-share/margin structure matched to the partner type.
2. The partner CAC calculation with assumptions marked, and the inputs the user must supply listed
   under "Still open".
3. The comparison to direct/blended CAC and the honest verdict (efficiency vs. reach).
4. The fully-loaded cost, not just the headline margin.
