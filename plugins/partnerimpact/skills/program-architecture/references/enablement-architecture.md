# Enablement Architecture

Enablement is the bridge between a signed partner and a producing one. Most programs treat it as a
one-time onboarding event: a kickoff call, a slide deck, a login. Then they wonder why activation
rates are low. Enablement is a ladder the partner climbs while producing, not a gate they pass once.

## The activation problem

A signed partner is a liability until they produce. The metric that matters is **time-to-first-
opportunity**: days from signed agreement to first qualified joint deal. Everything in the
enablement path exists to shorten it. If your enablement doesn't move that number, it's theater.

## The certification ladder

Structure enablement as levels, each tied to a capability the partner needs to sell or deliver, and
each earning something they want.

| Level | Partner can… | Earns |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundational** | Position the joint value proposition, qualify a fit | Deal registration, portal access |
| **Sales-certified** | Run a discovery, handle objections, co-sell a deal | Co-marketing, MDF eligibility, leads |
| **Technical-certified** | Scope, demo, and implement the integration/solution | Priority support, sandbox, solution listing |
| **Delivery-certified** | Own implementation and drive adoption | Delivery-led revenue, marquee references |

Not every partner needs every rung: a referral partner may stop at Foundational; an SI climbs to
Delivery. Map the ladder to the motion (see segmentation in `partner-frameworks`).

## The first-90-days path

The onboarding window is where partnerships are won or lost. A default arc:

- **Days 0–30: Orient.** JBP signed, joint value prop learned, first reps certified at
  Foundational, account mapping started. Exit: partner knows what to sell and to whom.
- **Days 31–60: Enable.** Sales certification, first co-marketing activity live, first accounts
  worked jointly. Exit: first qualified opportunity in the pipeline.
- **Days 61–90: Activate.** Deal support on the first opportunities, technical enablement as
  needed, first QBR scheduled. Exit: a producing partner with a rhythm.

A partner who produces nothing in this window rarely recovers momentum. Front-load the human touch
here, then step back.

## What good enablement content covers

- **The joint value proposition**, not your product pitch, the *combined* story and where it wins.
- **Qualification**: how the partner's reps spot a fit and hand it off cleanly.
- **Objection handling**: the real objections in joint deals, including "why not just buy direct."
- **The co-sell mechanics**: rules of engagement, deal registration, who does what (see the
  `co-sell-motion` skill).

## Enablement mistakes

- **Onboarding as an event**: a kickoff and a deck, then silence. Enablement is continuous.
- **Product training instead of joint-value training**: teaching partners your features instead of
  the combined story that sells.
- **No activation checkpoint**: enablement with no tie to first-opportunity is unaccountable.
- **Same path for every motion**: a referral partner drowned in technical certification, an SI
  starved of delivery enablement.

## Output of an enablement design

1. The certification ladder mapped to the partner's motion.
2. The first-90-days path with an exit criterion per phase.
3. The activation checkpoint (time-to-first-opportunity target).
4. The content priorities, joint-value first.
