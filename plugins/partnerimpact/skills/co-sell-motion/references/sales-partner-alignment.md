# Sales-Partner Alignment: the 3Is

A diagnostic for why your own sellers do or do not bring partners into deals. It is not about the
partner at all. When co-sell activity exists but commercial outcomes do not, the cause is almost
always one of three things, and they are not equally likely.

| Dimension | Diagnostic question | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| **Incentives** | Does the sales comp plan reward partner involvement? | Sales earns full commission either way, so there is no reason to share the deal |
| **Information** | Does sales know which partners to engage, and when? | A PAM list exists, but the rep cannot tell who to call or what the partner adds |
| **Involvement** | Is there a defined co-sell play the field knows how to run? | Partners get invited to demos with no scripted role, which is awkward rather than additive |

## How to use it

Ask the three questions in order and stop at the first one that fails. They compound downward: an
information fix cannot survive a broken incentive, and a well-designed play cannot survive either.

Most teams reach for Involvement first, because a play is the easiest of the three to build and the
only one a partnerships leader can ship without another function's approval. That is usually why the
motion stalls. Incentives sit with sales leadership and finance, Information sits with RevOps, and
those are the conversations worth having early rather than late.

When all three line up, partner-involved deals close faster, at higher ACV, with better retention.
When one is broken, you get activity that never converts, and the activity is what makes the problem
hard to see.

## Relationship to the three asks

Distinct framework, easily confused. [`cosell-process.md`](cosell-process.md) covers **the three
asks**: Intel, Influence and Intros, which is what a seller asks a partner *for* on a specific deal.
The 3Is here are about whether your own field is set up to make that ask at all. Diagnose alignment
first; the asks only matter once a rep has a reason to pick up the phone.

## What this does not diagnose

If all three hold and co-sell still underperforms, the problem is upstream: the partner cannot
deliver, the ICP overlap was never real, or the joint value proposition does not survive contact
with a customer. Hand it to `partner-diagnostician` rather than tuning the play again.
