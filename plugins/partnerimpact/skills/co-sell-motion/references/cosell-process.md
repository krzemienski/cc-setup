# The Co-sell Process: the three asks

Co-sell happens at the field level, rep to rep. Strip away the frameworks and the partner asks the
seller for one of three things on any given deal: **Intel, Influence, or Intros.** Naming the ask
keeps both sides aligned on what's being exchanged, and it prevents the vague "let's
collaborate" that produces nothing.

## The three asks

### Intel
What the partner knows that the seller doesn't: the account's tech stack, the real decision-maker,
the incumbent, the budget cycle, the political landmine. Intel de-risks a deal without either side
committing resources. It's the lowest-friction exchange and often the highest-leverage.

### Influence
The partner actively advocates: a reference call, a joint value story, a nudge to their champion
inside the account. Influence moves a deal you already have. It's most valuable mid-funnel, when the
customer is deciding, not discovering.

### Intros
The partner opens a door you can't open: to a new account, a new buying center, a more senior
sponsor. Intros are the highest-value and highest-cost ask; a partner spends relationship capital to
make one. Reserve them for deals that matter and reciprocate.

## Keeping partner and direct aligned

The failure mode is two sellers working the same account with different pictures. A working rhythm:

1. **Joint account map first**: both teams see overlap and whitespace (see account-mapping).
2. **Per-deal ownership named**: one seller owns the customer relationship; the other supports.
   Ambiguity here is where co-sell dies.
3. **The ask stated explicitly**: "I need intel on their AWS commitment," not "can you help."
4. **A shared next step**: every co-sell touch ends with who does what by when.
5. **Reciprocity tracked**: co-sell is a two-way ledger. A partner who only ever receives asks
   stops answering. Give intel and intros back.

## The incentive reality

A perfect co-sell process fails if the field isn't paid to run it. Before diagnosing process,
check:

- **Comp**: does the rep get quota credit for a co-sold deal, or does the partner "cost" them
  margin? If co-sell dents their comp, they'll route around it.
- **Routing**: do partner-sourced leads reach a rep who's measured on them, or die in a queue?
- **Friction**: is registering and working a partner deal easy, or a tax the rep avoids?

Co-sell is an incentives problem before it is a tooling problem. Fix comp and routing first.

## Output of a co-sell plan

1. The joint account map (overlap + whitespace + priority targets).
2. Per-target: the ask type (Intel/Influence/Intros) and the owner on each side.
3. Rules of engagement in force (see that reference).
4. The comp/routing check: is the field incentivized to run this.

## Before you tune the asks

If the field is not engaging partners at all, the asks are not the problem. Run the alignment
diagnostic in [`sales-partner-alignment.md`](sales-partner-alignment.md) first: incentives, then
information, then involvement. A well-designed ask cannot survive a comp plan that pays the rep the
same either way.
