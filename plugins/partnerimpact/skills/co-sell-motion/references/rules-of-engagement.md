# Rules of Engagement (RoE)

The document that prevents the fights that kill co-sell. RoE answers, in advance, the questions that
otherwise get answered mid-deal in an argument: who owns the customer, who does what, what happens
when both sides claim the same account, and how disputes get resolved. Written before the first
joint deal, it's cheap insurance. Written after the first fight, it's damage control.

## What RoE must decide

### 1. Customer ownership
Who owns the primary customer relationship: partner or vendor? This is the load-bearing decision.
Options:

- **Vendor-led**: vendor owns the customer; partner supports and is compensated for it.
- **Partner-led**: partner owns the customer and the transaction; vendor supports (common in
  reseller and SI motions).
- **Named per deal**: ownership assigned deal by deal via account mapping and registration.

Pick a default and name the exceptions. Ambiguity here is the single biggest source of channel
conflict.

### 2. Roles in the deal
Who runs discovery, who demos, who scopes, who negotiates commercials, who owns the close. Map it to
the motion: a referral partner hands off early; an SI may run the whole cycle with vendor support.

### 3. Boundaries
- **Segments/accounts reserved for direct** vs. open to partners.
- **Whether partners can sell to existing direct customers** (and how expansion is handled).
- **Geographic or vertical exclusivity**, if any (grant sparingly: exclusivity is expensive).

### 4. Conflict resolution
- **The default rule** when two claims collide (usually: first valid registration wins; see deal
  registration).
- **The escalation path**: who adjudicates, in what timeframe, with what authority.
- **The tiebreaker principle**: decide once, in the RoE, what governs (evidence of sourcing,
  customer preference, strategic value), so escalations are fast and consistent.

## Channel conflict: the real sources

RoE prevents conflict only if it names its actual causes:

- **Direct vs. partner on the same deal**: the classic. Solved by ownership rules + registration.
- **Partner vs. partner on the same account**: two partners claim the same customer. Solved by
  registration precedence and, where needed, account assignment.
- **Comp-driven conflict**: the rep is paid to route around the partner. No RoE fixes a broken comp
  plan; flag it to the economics/comp owner.
- **Pricing conflict**: partner and direct quote the same customer different prices. Solved by
  pricing discipline and registration protection.

## The principle behind good RoE

RoE should be **short, clear, and biased toward letting deals move.** Rules so restrictive that reps
route around them are worse than no rules. The goal is fast, predictable answers to ownership and
conflict, not a legal document nobody reads. If a rep can't tell you in one sentence who owns a
given account, the RoE has failed.

## Output of an RoE

1. Customer ownership default + named exceptions.
2. Role assignment by motion type.
3. Boundaries (reserved segments, existing-customer rules, exclusivity if any).
4. The conflict-resolution default, escalation path, and tiebreaker principle.
