# Referral / Agency

The lightest motion and the most over-recruited. A referral partner passes you a lead and steps
back. Cheap to sign, which is exactly why most referral programs are a spreadsheet of dormant
names.

## Qualification

**Dimensions that carry the weight:** market access above everything, reach into accounts you
don't already touch is the entire value. Commitment signal second: does anyone there have a reason
to remember you exist.

**Dimensions that matter less:** technical fit is close to irrelevant. Delivery capability doesn't
apply. They aren't delivering anything.

**Type-specific anti-fit:** they already refer to a direct competitor and won't stop. Their client
relationship is transactional enough that a referral carries no trust. They want a fee for
introductions they were making anyway.

The question that qualifies a referral partner: *what happens in their business that makes
your product the obvious next conversation?* If there isn't a natural trigger, referrals won't
flow no matter what the fee is.

## Economics

Money moves one way: you pay them. Usually a percentage of first-year contract value, sometimes a
flat fee per qualified opportunity.

- **Percentage of first-year value** is standard and self-limiting. They get paid when you do.
- **Flat fee per qualified lead** pays faster and attracts volume, including junk. Only use it with
  a hard qualification gate, and expect to police it.
- Partner CAC here should be well under blended CAC. If it isn't, the referral fee is too high or
  the leads are too weak: check which before renewing the agreement.

Watch the double-payment trap: a referral fee on an account your own team was already working is
pure margin loss. This is what deal registration is for, even in a motion this light.

## Enablement

Minimal, and that's the point. Over-enabling a referral partner is a common waste. They don't need
product training, they need to recognize the trigger and know how to hand off.

**Activated means:** one qualified referral submitted and accepted. Not trained, not certified.

**What they need:** a one-page description of the trigger, the submission path (make it
take under two minutes), and fast acknowledgement. Slow acknowledgement kills referral programs
faster than low fees.

**Realistic time to first referral:** weeks, not quarters. If nothing arrives in 60 days, the
trigger isn't real for them and no amount of follow-up will change it.

## Co-sell

There generally isn't one, and pretending otherwise wastes everyone's time. The partner hands over
and exits. Occasionally they'll join a first call for warmth: treat that as a favor, not a motion.

What matters instead is the **handoff quality and the loop back**. Tell them what happened to their
referral. Partners who never hear the outcome stop sending them, and that single failure kills more
referral programs than commercial terms ever do.
