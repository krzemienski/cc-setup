# Marketplace

Distribution through someone else's storefront: a cloud marketplace, an app store, an ecosystem
directory. The partner here is often a platform rather than a company with a partner manager, which
changes the relationship fundamentally: you cannot negotiate with a marketplace the way you
negotiate with a reseller.

## Qualification

You're qualifying a **channel**, not a counterparty. The dimensions shift accordingly.

**What carries the weight:** market access, are your buyers there, and do they buy this
way. Commercial motion fit: does the marketplace's transaction model match how you sell (a
self-serve listing under an enterprise motion produces listings nobody transacts). Economic
viability, because the marketplace takes a cut you don't control.

**What matters less:** commitment signal in the usual sense. The marketplace isn't committing to
you; you're committing to it.

**Type-specific anti-fit:** the take rate makes the economics negative at your price point. The
marketplace's own first-party product competes with yours and gets ranking preference. Listing
requirements demand engineering work disproportionate to the addressable volume. Your buyer doesn't
have procurement access to that marketplace.

The question that qualifies a marketplace: *does listing here reach a buyer we can't reach, or does
it just move existing deals into someone else's checkout at a worse margin?* Committed-spend
drawdown on cloud marketplaces is the common legitimate answer: the buyer has budget already
committed to the platform and can only spend it there.

## Economics

The take rate is the headline and rarely the whole story.

- **Platform fee / take rate**: typically a percentage of transaction value, sometimes reducing at
  volume or with a private offer.
- **Private offers** change the math substantially on large deals and are where most real
  marketplace revenue lands.
- **Committed-spend drawdown** is the real economic driver on cloud marketplaces: the customer
  spends budget they've already committed to the platform, which shortens procurement dramatically.
  That's worth real margin.
- Listing and maintenance cost is engineering time, ongoing.

Attribution needs care. A marketplace transaction where your own team sourced and closed the deal
is not marketplace-sourced revenue: the marketplace was the payment rail. Distinguish
marketplace-**sourced** from marketplace-**transacted** in the crediting policy, or the numbers will
flatter the channel and misdirect investment.

## Enablement

You are the one being enabled. The work is on your side, not theirs.

- **Listing quality**: the listing is a landing page competing with every other listing. Most are
  neglected after launch.
- **Discoverability and ranking**: categories, keywords, reviews, and whatever the platform's
  ranking rewards. Review volume compounds; start collecting early.
- **Internal enablement**: your own sellers need to know when routing a deal through the
  marketplace helps the customer. Most sellers avoid it because it complicates their comp. Fix the
  comp treatment or the channel stays dormant.

**Activated means:** transactions flowing, not a live listing. A listing with no transactions after
two quarters is a maintenance liability.

**Realistic time to first transaction:** a quarter or more for the listing and its approvals, then
however long your normal sales cycle runs.

## Co-sell

Two very different things get called marketplace co-sell.

**Self-serve listings:** no co-sell. The motion is discovery and conversion, and it's a marketing
problem.

**Cloud marketplace co-sell programs:** real, and worth using. The platform's own field team can influence
or source deals, and the programs that matter have their own registration mechanics, partner tiers,
and seller incentives. This is a serious motion with its own rules, and it only works if you engage
the platform's field organization rather than just maintaining a listing.

Treat the platform's sellers as a sales team you must earn attention from. They have a portfolio and
you're one of hundreds of listings in it. What earns their attention is a clear customer problem,
an easy transaction path, and a partner who shows up with named accounts rather than asking for
leads.
