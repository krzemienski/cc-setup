# Reseller / VAR

The partner owns the commercial relationship and sells your product as their own line item. The
highest-control motion for them, the lowest for you, and the one where channel conflict is not a
risk to manage but a certainty to design around.

## Qualification

**Dimensions that carry the weight:** commercial motion fit, do their deal sizes, sales cycle, and
buyer match yours. Delivery capability, because the customer's experience is now theirs. Economic
viability, because margin has to work for both sides at realistic volume.

**Dimensions that matter less:** technical fit, unless they're also integrating.

**Type-specific anti-fit:** they resell a directly competing product and yours becomes the fallback
quote. Their margin expectation is incompatible with your pricing floor. They want exclusivity in a
territory they can't cover. They intend to resell without supporting. You'll inherit the support
load with none of the revenue.

The question that qualifies a reseller: *why is selling your product better for them than selling
what they sell now?* "Additional revenue" is not an answer. Attach rate to their existing motion is.

## Economics

Money moves through them. You sell at a discount; they sell at list or their own price.

- **Margin bands by tier** are the standard structure: higher discount for higher commitment,
  volume, or certification. Get the bands right at design time; clawing margin back later is a
  relationship event.
- **The floor matters more than the band.** Set the discount your business can survive at scale,
  then work up. Resellers negotiate every renewal.
- **Deal registration protects the margin**, not just the deal. Without it, two resellers quote the
  same customer and the only variable left is your discount.
- Partner CAC looks excellent here and hides real cost: enablement, support escalation, and the
  margin itself. Compare fully loaded, not on headcount alone.

## Enablement

The heaviest of the six. They're selling and often supporting, so they need real capability.

**Activated means:** first deal closed independently, without your rep in the room. That's the
whole point of the motion, and it's the checkpoint most programs never test.

**What they need:** sales enablement (pitch, objections, competitive), commercial enablement
(pricing, quoting, deal reg), and support enablement (tier-1 handling, escalation path). A
certification with teeth, because their reps represent you.

**Realistic time to first independent deal:** one to two quarters. Anyone promising faster is
counting a deal your team closed.

## Co-sell

Real, but transitional. Early deals are effectively co-sold: your rep in the room, their
relationship. The motion is designed to work itself out of that: each deal should need less of you.

**Track the weaning.** A reseller still needing your rep on every deal after four quarters is not a
reseller; they're a referral partner on reseller margin, and the economics are wrong.

Rules of engagement matter more here than anywhere else. Who owns the customer, who prices, who
renews, and what happens when your direct team finds the same account. Write it down before the
first conflict, not after.
